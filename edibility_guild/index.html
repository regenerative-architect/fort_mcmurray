A cold-hardy edible guild around existing apples.

This planner prioritizes plants likely to tolerate Fort McMurray's short, cold season while adding edible yield, pollinator continuity, groundcover, habitat, or soil support without treating the apple root zone as spare digging space. The strongest public-space default is a low-disturbance inner ring of chives + strawberry, with haskap/currant in brighter outer positions and larger shrubs placed well beyond paths and major apple roots.

Site strategy: protect the existing apple first

RingPlacementBest use
0 · trunk0–0.6 mMulch only. Keep trunk flare uncovered. No digging, raised soil, or irrigation hardware against bark.
1 · shallow guild~0.6–1.5 mSmall plugs/divisions: chives, wild/alpine strawberry, sorrel where light allows. Hand tools only.
2 · productive edge~1.5–3 mHaskap, compact currants, gooseberry only where root excavation can be kept modest and access remains clear.
3 · outer resilience3 m+ / separate bedSaskatoon, raspberry, hazelnut, squashberry; aggressive or large plants only in bounded perimeter beds.

Why: mature apple roots extend beyond the canopy. Large planting holes sever roots and can create competition. Top-dressing with compost and adding shallow plugs is safer than excavating a dense “permaculture guild” directly beneath an established tree.

Hospital-ground constraints

  • Snow + salt: avoid snow-dump zones and curb edges receiving de-icer. Salt stress can overwhelm otherwise hardy plants.
  • Food safety: confirm no incompatible pesticide/herbicide regime; do not assume ornamental fruit is managed as food.
  • Slip / access: keep dropped berries, nuts, canes and irrigation off sidewalks, ambulance access and accessibility routes.
  • Wildlife: berries and fallen apples can attract birds and mammals. Use harvest/cleanup schedules; avoid high-attraction thickets near entrances.
  • Clinical context: prefer low-thorn, non-toxic-to-touch species near patient paths. Rhubarb leaves are toxic if eaten; thorny/suckering species need separation.
  • Pollinators: flowering herbs help, but avoid dense bee forage immediately beside doors or high-traffic seating if facilities staff identify sting-risk concerns.
Recommended starting geometryFor one mature apple: preserve a mulch-only trunk zone; trial 6–10 chive clumps + 8–16 strawberry plugs in the brighter half of the outer root zone; place one compact currant or a compatible pair of haskaps beyond the densest roots if space allows. Observe one full season before adding a larger layer.

Planting sequence

  1. Map before digging: trunk, dripline, irrigation, utilities, paths, snow piles, sunny sectors and current mulch depth.
  2. Get owner approval: confirm AHS/site-facilities scope, who maintains/harvests, whether public picking is permitted, and acceptable species.
  3. Test the soil: pH, organic matter, salinity/EC if road-salt exposure is plausible, and contaminants if the bed history is uncertain.
  4. Start shallow: add compost as a surface top-dress, then small plugs/divisions. Do not rototill beneath established apples.
  5. Water establishment, not dependence: deep, infrequent watering in year 1 when dry; taper once perennial roots establish.
  6. Mulch: wood chips around woody plants; keep crowns/stems clear. Straw works well for strawberry winter protection.
  7. Observe one winter: record dieback, snow load, salt injury, sun/shade and maintenance conflicts before scaling.

Seasonal timing for Fort McMurray

Late winter / early springOrder bare-root shrubs/crowns; seek approvals; map roots and utilities.
As soil becomes workablePlant dormant bare-root shrubs and divisions; spring planting is preferred for many woody edibles.
After hard frost risk easesTransplant herbs/annual flowers and tender nursery stock; mulch and water.
June–AugustHarvest early crops, weed lightly, keep fruit off paths, record pollinator and wildlife activity.
Late summerStop pushing tender late growth with nitrogen; continue watering woody plants during drought.
After dormancyWinter mulch strawberries; protect young shrubs from browsing; document snow-storage effects.

Cost & sourcing strategy

Costs in the catalog are planning ranges in CAD for Canadian retail/online nursery material in 2026, not quotes. Live-plant inventory in Fort McMurray is strongly seasonal, so call ahead. Current local places with garden/landscape inventory include the Canadian Tire main store at 1 Hospital Street, RONA Fort McMurray, and McMurray Landscape Center; specialty cold-hardy fruit often requires a Canadian mail-order nursery. The closest store is not automatically the best genetic material—cultivar hardiness matters more.

Buy locally when…

  • You can inspect roots/canes and avoid shipping stress.
  • The tag clearly identifies cultivar + hardiness zone.
  • You need simple plugs, herbs, strawberries, mulch or soil amendments.
  • You can confirm the plant has been hardened for northern conditions.

Mail-order when…

  • You need named haskap pollination partners, disease-resistant currants/gooseberries, native shrubs or beaked hazelnut.
  • Local tags only say “assorted berry.”
  • You want economical bare-root bundles.
  • Order timing lets dormant stock arrive before active growth.
Price reality checkRecent Canadian examples found during research included roughly $20 raspberry plants, $10–35 Saskatoon stock, $14–20 gooseberries, ~$43 beaked hazelnut, and a two-cultivar haskap container listed around $59. Treat these as reference points only; shipping, pot size and seasonal stock can move prices substantially.

Zero-harm / anti-inversion note

This design is meant to increase local edible resilience without converting a public healthcare landscape into an unmanaged experiment. The safety hierarchy is: people and clinical access → existing trees/soil → ecological function → harvest. A species that is excellent on an acreage may be deliberately ranked low beside a hospital walkway.
